Chapter 1 · Learn the building blocks

Foundations of Paragraph Structure

A strong paragraph focuses on one main idea and supports it with relevant details.

1. The three key parts

Topic sentence: introduces the main idea and controlling opinion.

Supporting details: explain, illustrate, or give reasons for the topic sentence.

Concluding sentence: brings the paragraph to a close by restating the main point in a fresh way.

Topic sentence: Social media can support students' learning when they use it purposefully.

Supporting detail 1: Students can join online study groups to share notes.

Supporting detail 2: Teachers can post useful videos and learning resources.

Supporting detail 3: Classmates can discuss difficult topics after school.

Concluding sentence: Therefore, purposeful social media use can make learning more collaborative.

2. Try it: identify the supporting detail

Topic sentence: “Social media platforms can improve collaborative learning.” Which sentence best supports it?

Chapter 2 · Plan before you draft

Thesis Statements & Essay Outlines

A thesis statement gives your essay a clear position. An outline helps each paragraph support that position.

1. From topic to thesis

Topic: social media for learning

Weak thesis: Social media is an interesting topic.

Stronger thesis: Social media should be used as a learning tool because it helps students share resources, discuss ideas, and receive feedback.

A useful thesis states your position and previews the main reasons you will explain.

2. Simple argumentative essay outline

  1. Introduction: introduce the topic and state the thesis.
  2. Body paragraph 1: explain reason one with evidence or an example.
  3. Body paragraph 2: explain reason two with evidence or an example.
  4. Conclusion: restate the position and summarize the key reasons.

Chapter 3 · Make ideas flow

Cohesion, Transitions & AI-Assisted Drafting

Transitions show the relationship between ideas. Cohesion helps readers follow your message.

1. Common transition words

Addition

Furthermore, in addition, also

Contrast

However, in contrast, on the other hand

Cause and effect

Therefore, as a result, consequently

Example

For example, for instance

Punctuation tip: When “however” connects two independent clauses, use a semicolon before it and a comma after it: “Social media is useful; however, it can distract students.”

2. Responsible AI-assisted drafting

  1. Write your own ideas and first draft.
  2. Ask an AI tool to identify grammar, clarity, or cohesion issues.
  3. Review each suggestion and decide whether it preserves your intended meaning.
  4. Verify facts and examples; AI can make mistakes.
  5. Keep your own voice and follow your teacher’s rules for AI use.
Prompt you can adapt:

“Please give feedback on my paragraph for grammar, clarity, and transition words. Do not rewrite the whole paragraph. Explain each suggestion and let me decide what to change.”

3. Quick practice

Choose the best transition: “Online platforms provide access to learning resources. ________, they allow students to study flexibly.”

Chapter 4 · Learn from feedback

Peer Review & Feedback Integration

Peer review helps writers see whether their ideas are clear to another reader.

1. Use the “Two Stars and a Wish” method

  • Star 1: identify one strength in the argument or organization.
  • Star 2: identify a second strength, such as a clear example or transition.
  • Wish: suggest one specific improvement the writer can make.

2. Peer-review checklist

Use this checklist in Google Docs comments or when reviewing a partner’s draft.

3. Give helpful feedback

Less helpful: “Your paragraph is confusing.”

More helpful: “Your second reason is interesting. Could you add an example to show how an online study group helps students learn?”

Be kind, specific, and focused on the writing—not the writer.
